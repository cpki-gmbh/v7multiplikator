Power-saving benchmark

What happens to each engine when the CPU clocks drop. Measured on the same laptop, the same four models, and the same five-round protocol as page 1 — but with the Windows power plan set to Power saver for the entire run.

November 10, 2026 · tuner-loop cycle throttled clocks · all engines slower v7 keeps 93 % of its speed · the field keeps 83 %

What happened, and why it is interesting

One benchmark-loop cycle (build databases, run the full four-model suite, repeat) was started with the laptop still in the Windows Power saver plan instead of the usual high-performance state. Every engine ran with throttled clocks, back to back, twice: once with v7's in-process tuner running (delay 0) and once with the tuner silent (delay 99999). The tuner-silent run is the honest power-saving figure and is what this page shows.

An accident — but a useful one. It answers a question the full-power benchmarks cannot: how gracefully does each engine degrade when the CPU slows down? That is exactly the condition of a laptop on battery, a fanless box in a warm room, or any eco power profile.

v7's workers spin-wait on their own physical cores and hand work through shared memory — that scales down linearly with the clock. llama.cpp and OpenVINO pay extra for every thread handoff and synchronization, and those fixed costs hurt more as the clock drops. Shorter bar = smaller loss.

The tuner across the loop Qwen3-0.6B short chat, three consecutive legs

Three legs, one story

1 · tuner on, fresh
48.5
2 · tuner quiet
52.0
3 · tuner on again
51.0
The first leg (tuner learning from scratch, changing configurations mid-run) is 7 % slower than the quiet leg. The third leg — tuner on again, but starting from what leg 1 learned — nearly matches the quiet speed.

Why that matters

v7's tuner stores every improvement it finds. After one learning cycle the tuned configurations are already in place, and even a running tuner costs almost nothing extra. Two practical rules follow: benchmark with the tuner quiet (delay 99999) for clean numbers, and let the tuner run in production — its work carries over through the persisted populations and keeps paying off.
Machine state: all three legs throttled the same way, and the non-v7 engines reproduced within about 1 % across them, so the v7 movement between legs is the tuner's, not the machine's.

Bottom line

Under power saving v7 keeps 93 % of its full-power throughput while llama.cpp and OpenVINO keep 83 %, so v7's lead widens as the CPU slows — from +7 % to +21 % on Qwen3-0.6B short chat, and enough to flip two Phi-3-mini workloads outright. For battery, eco-profile, or thermally constrained deployments, v7 is the engine that degrades least.
